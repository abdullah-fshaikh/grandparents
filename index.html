The Journeys of Mukhtar & Shehnaz
1975 — 2016 · A flight memoir

Mukhtar & Shehnaz

VAGHLI · BOMBAY · TRIPOLI · LONDON · HONG KONG · JEDDAH · ST. LOUIS

On the 24th of February, 1975, a young man from Vaghli boarded an Air India flight from Bombay to Beirut. For the next forty years — with Shehnaz beside him, and in time their sons Faheem and Faeez — he crossed the world again and again, and wrote every flight down. This page keeps his record, exactly as he kept it.

Five chapters

The shape of a life, told in boarding passes

1975–1976Chapter one

First departures

Bombay to Beirut, Beirut on to Tripoli. Mukhtar was thirty years old when he first left India, and within two years he had already looped through London, Rome, Hong Kong and Taipei. Then, in November 1976, a second passport received its first stamps: Shehnaz flew Bombay–Athens–Tripoli, and from that day forward the record is a story of two.

1977–1984Chapter two

The Libya years

Tripoli was home base, London the constant shuttle, Bombay the anchor. Business carried Mukhtar east to Hong Kong, Seoul, Taipei, Singapore and Manila, and south to Lagos. In 1979 the flights gain a small new passenger — baby Faheem — and by 1981, Faeez. In 1982 and 1983 the whole family flew to Jeddah for Umrah, once returning overland through Karachi and Delhi. In 1983 alone, Mukhtar recorded more than fifty take-offs.

1986–1987Chapter three

Family voyages

The trading years soften into travel taken together: Hong Kong and Bangkok with the boys in the spring of 1986, Hong Kong and Taipei again in 1987. And that December, a journey above all others — Umrah to Jeddah with Amma, mother, wife and sons on one aeroplane.

1995Chapter four

The long return

February: Mukhtar and Shehnaz make Umrah together, just the two of them. Then one last great solo circle — Bangkok, Hong Kong, Rome, Malta, and a ship across the water to Tripoli, the old city, one final time. He spent a season in Warsaw, reached Moscow, Minsk and Prague, and came home to Bombay in November. He never crossed those borders again.

2007–2016Chapter five

America, together

The sons settled in America, and so the map folds itself around them: every flight now made side by side — Bombay to Florence to Atlanta in 2007, and then years of the gentlest route in the whole book, St. Louis ↔ Birmingham, between Faeez’s home and Faheem’s. The last entry in Shehnaz’s log is not a flight at all: July 2016, St. Louis to Birmingham, by car, with Faeez’s family. The travellers were home.

Between the lines — journeys the airlines never logged
A day in Tel AvivFrom Athens to Israel and back in a single day, 1978 — on temporary papers valid for one journey, three days.
By ship to TripoliIn 1995 the airports of Libya were closed to the world, so he sailed from Malta — and sailed back thirteen days later.
By road, again and againTripoli to Tunis by car, three times. Port Said to the Israeli border, five or six. Larnaca to the Turkish border by jeep, with Arjan.
Nights without a visaAn overnight in Istanbul on the airline’s account; fourteen hours in Zurich the same way. Twice by car from Warsaw to the German border.
India, by airWith Shehnaz and the boys: Pune–Delhi–Srinagar and back — Kashmir with the children — and twice to Delhi alone for visas.
